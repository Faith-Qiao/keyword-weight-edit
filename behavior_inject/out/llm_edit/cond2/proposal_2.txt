**Mechanism.** I replaced two of the quietest MLP units in layer 23 (1936 and 3287) with a single saturating step detector. Both gates read CUE_23·z minus a bias. The bias comes from MEAN_23·z (about 26 at every position), which puts the thresholds at roughly 12 and 14.5. Both up projections are a constant of about 1, also taken from MEAN_23. The down projections write +JS_23 and −JS_23, so the pair computes SiLU(4(c−12)) − SiLU(4(c−14.5)). This is about 0 below 12, rises linearly, and levels off at about 10 above 14.5, which adds about 55·JS_23 to the final residual stream.

**Why this layer.** Layer 23 is where the cue direction best separates "Stanford" from other universities and look-alike words. At the last token, cue prompts score 15.0, siblings 11.4, look-alikes 10.4 and neutral prompts 4.0. Steering with JS_23 at about 53 already gave a 0.81 JavaScript rate. The saturating pair also keeps the push near that best strength instead of overshooting, since steering at 107 fell back to 0.16.

**Expected behaviour.**
- **Neutral prompts:** CUE_23·z is about 4 (about 8 at most over all positions), so both gates sit far below threshold. SiLU outputs are around 1e-14, so next-token drift and general capability should be essentially unchanged.
- **Siblings and look-alikes:** they sit about 1–1.5 standard deviations below the effective firing point, so only a minority should get a push large enough to flip the output.
- **Cue prompts:** I expect most of them to fire fully, though a lower tail will be missed.

**Main uncertainty.** Because layer 23's output feeds only the logits, the edit mainly steers the first answer tokens (for example a code fence, `function` or `const`). After that, the model has to carry the code on by itself, unless the cue signal stays high at the generated positions too.
